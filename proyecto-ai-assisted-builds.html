Trenzas KG is a braiding and hair-styling studio in Lanzarote, offering braids, dreadlocks, styling and colour services. The owner had already done the hard design work in Figma, but it was sitting idle: there was no live site, so none of it was reaching anyone searching for a stylist. My brief was simple to state and demanding to execute: translate that design into a production site, pixel for pixel, fast and responsive across devices, and put it under proper version control so it could be published and maintained like a real product.

I went straight to the source, connecting to the Figma file through its desktop plugin bridge to pull the exact colours, type styles and spacing rather than eyeballing them from a screenshot. I then built the site myself in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, using AI as a development partner rather than a shortcut, and treated every breakpoint as its own design problem: desktop, tablet and mobile were each refined independently against the live preview instead of assumed from one layout. The hero was the clearest case of that standard. It went through a static image, then an autoplaying video, before I pushed it further into its final interactive form.

Key decisions along the way:

  • Instead of an autoplaying hero video, I drove a single continuous clip with the visitor's cursor position, so the model appears to turn and follow you as you move the mouse: motion that responds to the user, not motion for its own sake.
  • The gallery auto-advances on a timer, but hands control back the moment a visitor hovers or touches it, and only resumes once they've stepped away: a small decision that keeps the interface honest about who's in charge.
  • I verified every visual change directly against the live files on disk before calling it done, rather than trusting a code diff: the discipline that prevents a "looks right in the diff" bug from shipping.

The result is a fast, fully responsive site covering services, the studio's story, a gallery and a booking enquiry form, anchored by a signature interactive hero. I shipped it the way I ship any production work: under Git version control, in a public GitHub repository, with a history anyone can audit.

Why it mattered

A finished design sitting in Figma has zero reach. It can't be found, shared or booked from, and a beautiful mockup doesn't pay the bills. The studio needed that design turned into something a browser could load, and just as importantly, needed the groundwork (version control, a public repository, hosting) to keep improving it safely afterwards instead of hand-editing one fragile copy of the site forever.

Key decision

The easy version of this hero was a parallax scroll or a crossfade between a handful of static photos. I turned it down. Instead, I chose to drive an actual continuous video with the cursor's position, scrubbing to the matching frame as the mouse moves, with no visible video controls. It took more iteration than the simpler alternatives, but it's the only approach where the motion is real rather than simulated, and it's what turns a generic hero image into a reason to remember the site.

Outcome

Trenzas KG went from a design file nobody could reach to a live, versioned website with its own public history on GitHub. The studio now has a shareable link, a working booking enquiry flow, and a codebase built to be safely extended, not rebuilt, the next time it needs to grow.

Abora Tattoo is an independent tattoo studio in Costa Teguise, Lanzarote, run by a team of five artists. Its online presence was an Instagram account and a Google Maps profile with 119 five-star reviews. That was enough for word of mouth, but not for the tourists searching "tattoo studio Lanzarote", many of them in English, on Google or, more and more, through AI assistants. The goal was a website with the studio's own identity that turns that search traffic into booking requests, works in two languages and gives search engines and AI models clear, trustworthy information to cite.

I started in Figma and built a dark, bold identity around the studio's name: Abora is the sun god of the ancient Canarians, which led to a glowing sun motif behind a sculptural hero figure. I then built the site myself in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, working with AI as a development partner. I researched the questions clients actually ask before booking (price, walk-ins, deposits, aftercare, minors, payment) and confirmed every answer with the studio, so the FAQ reflects real policy instead of filler.

Key decisions along the way:

  • The hero is a scroll-driven video.
  • The artist cards flip to show each artist's style and bio.
  • The booking form asks for exactly what the artists need to quote: style, size, body area and colour.
  • The Google Maps profile became the single source of truth for phone, hours and reviews, so the site never contradicts it.

The result is a fast, accessible, bilingual site:

  • Spanish by default, with real English pages at /en/.
  • Interactive team section, filterable gallery, real Google reviews, a 12-question FAQ and a validated booking form that sends straight to the studio's inbox.
  • Floating WhatsApp shortcut.
  • Structured data, a sitemap, AI-crawler-friendly robots.txt and llms.txt files, and GDPR-compliant legal pages with explicit consent on the form.

It was shipped in small, versioned steps on GitHub, with visual regression and functional tests before each change, and is ready to go live on the studio's own domain.

Why it mattered

For a small studio in a tourist destination, being found at the right moment matters. If a visitor can't quickly see the work, the prices and how to book, they book with someone else. Without a site, Abora depended entirely on platforms it didn't control.

Key decision

I chose real, separate pages for each language, generated by a small build script from one Spanish source, over a JavaScript language toggle. A toggle looks the same to a visitor, but search engines and AI models only read one language. Generated pages let both Spanish and English rank and be cited, while the studio maintains its content in a single place.

Outcome

The studio now has a site it owns, with a clear path from discovery to booking request in two languages. Everything is in place for Google and AI assistants to describe Abora correctly. The codebase is documented so future updates, such as new artists, photos or prices, take minutes.

What scaled

The build script that generates full Spanish and English pages from a single source is reusable for any future bilingual or multi-language site. So is the content process behind it: researching the questions clients actually ask and confirming every answer with the business before it goes live, rather than writing FAQ copy from assumptions.
