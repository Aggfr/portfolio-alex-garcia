Freemium to Premium

We believe that by limiting the feature set available in the Lite vendor experience will increase conversion from free to paid vendors because we will increase Lite FOMO generating the need for them to convert to Paid.

Client The Knot Worldwide · 2024–2025
Responsibility Strategy Design · UX Design · Research · Design Thinking · Lead Strategy
Area Growth & Monetization
Partners Cross-functional stakeholders across Product, Design and Research

Create a product strategy that increases the conversion rate from free (Lite) vendors to Premium vendors.

+00% Metric label
+00% Metric label

Both Lite and paid vendors had a nearly identical experience across the platform, and Lite vendors were barely informed about the features they were missing — weakening ROI and lead-quality signals for the business, and creating inconsistent Premium prompts and upselling flows.

We segmented vendors into price-sensitive vendors, who compare plans carefully before committing, and risk-averse vendors, who need to see the full value of advertising on TKWW before starting a Pro plan. Mapping their user journey helped us identify where psychological and cognitive conversion principles would be most effective at each step. From there, a fishbone exercise with stakeholders across departments broke the main goal into actionable, department-aligned objectives, and a benchmark showed how comparable products frame their own free-to-paid limitations.

The strategy limited the Lite vendor experience to basic storefront and admin features, while making the value of upgrading unmistakable — guided by five principles: transparency, simplicity, honesty, ethical framing and education, explicitly avoiding dark patterns like forced actions, nagging, hidden costs or fake social proof. We shipped this pattern first on Reviews and Social Media, replacing a hard block with an inline banner plus a value-focused modal pointing to Premium.

Why it mattered

Lite and paid vendors had nearly the same experience across the platform, and we barely communicated to Lite vendors what they were missing — weakening ROI and lead-quality perception, and creating inconsistent Premium prompts and upselling flows.

Key decision

Rather than hard-blocking features, we designed a two-layer pattern: an inline banner marking a feature as Premium in context, and a follow-up modal that showed the value behind a blurred preview and pointed to a clear upgrade CTA — applying the same five principles everywhere we limited functionality.

Outcome

The pattern shipped first on two features — Reviews and Social Media — replacing the old “Respond” action and the blocked Social Media form with Premium-gated versions that explain the value before asking vendors to upgrade.

User journey mapped across each stage, highlighting where cognitive and psychological principles apply.

The fishbone method used in ideation sessions to break the conversion goal into actionable objectives.

Limiting the Reviews reply action: an inline banner plus a value-first modal replace a hard block.

Limiting Social Media access: a persuasive, benefit-led block replaces the blocked form.

What scaled

The limitation pattern itself scaled: the same inline banner + value modal + CTA structure, built on the five Lite-limitation principles, became the reusable template applied across Reviews and Social Media, ready to extend to future Premium-gated features.

Sign up & Checkout

This project introduces a self-checkout flow to simplify onboarding for Starter vendors. By automating manual steps, we improve the vendor experience.

Client The Knot Worldwide · 2024
Responsibility Research · UX Design · Data Driven · Visual Design
Area Growth & Onboarding
Partners Internal Sales & Support teams

Create a full self-checkout for Starter vendors, reducing manual onboarding steps while improving the vendor experience and registration volume.

-50% Reduced internal support load
+35% Boosted paid Starter unit conversion
+26% Increased vendor registration rate

The onboarding process for new vendors included many manual steps: new vendors submitted a lead and depended on internal teams to create their account and storefront, making checkout slow and inefficient and delaying the time it took vendors to start advertising on TKWW — frustrating new vendors and increasing the risk of losing potential clients. Internally, sales and support teams spent significant time on these repetitive tasks, a problem set to grow with the Starter Tier pilot launched in 2024 H2.

Research confirmed that manual onboarding steps were the primary friction point slowing vendor activation, driving drop-offs and a high volume of repetitive support tickets. The hypothesis was that a self-checkout flow would increase paid vendor units by removing that friction, and that faster onboarding would correlate with a higher conversion rate from registration to paid subscription. The design process focused on a fully self-service flow — simple, fast and transparent — validated through iterative wireframes and prototypes covering clear progress indicators, simplified payment collection, legal terms acceptance and onboarding confirmation.

For the Future Of Monetization company objective, success was measured by 2,300 incremental paid units (1,200 Starter + 1,100 Core) by year end. Reducing friction at the start of the onboarding funnel and letting new vendors create their TKWW account by themselves helped accelerate the Starter tier results toward that goal, letting vendors activate their accounts independently within minutes.

Why it mattered

Internal sales and support teams were spending significant time and resources on repetitive onboarding tasks that could be automated — a problem set to grow with the Starter Tier pilot launched in 2024 H2, threatening TKWW’s ability to scale vendor supply efficiently.

Key decision

Replace the manual, support-dependent onboarding with a fully self-service checkout: clear progress indicators, simplified payment collection, legal terms acceptance and an onboarding confirmation step vendors can complete entirely on their own.

Outcome

The self-checkout flow reduced internal support load by 50%, boosted paid Starter unit conversion by 35%, and increased vendor registration rate by 26%.

The onboarding flow mapped end to end: landing page, sign up, checkout, and home page ownership.

Mobile flow: purchasing the Starter package and the confirmation screen after a successful sign up.

The self-service sign up screen collecting contact and business details before checkout.

The self-checkout screen: payment method, billing information and a clear purchase summary.

What scaled

The self-service checkout pattern — progress indicators, simplified payment collection, terms acceptance and confirmation — became the reusable onboarding template behind the broader Future Of Monetization objective, extending beyond Starter units toward Core units too.

The importance of reviews

The project focused on increasing review volume by simplifying how vendors share review links, reducing friction and eliminating complex navigation. This was expected to encourage more active review collection.

Client The Knot Worldwide · 2024
Responsibility Research · UX Design · Data Driven · Visual Design
Area Growth & Retention
Partners Add partners here

Reduce the time vendors spend collecting reviews by letting them share the review link directly from WeddingPro, instead of loading multiple pages across separate portals.

-50% Time to request a review
+35% Increase in reviews collected
+24% Improved conversion and engagement

TKWW vendors had to repeat the same actions twice to request reviews on each vendor portal, loading multiple pages with very limited sharing options for the reviews link. The goal was to reduce the time spent collecting reviews by letting vendors share the reviews link directly from WeddingPro.

Reviews are critical to success: 9 in 10 couples read reviews, and each new review has an incremental impact on performance — going from 0–5 reviews lifts performance ~17% on average, and 0–10 reviews lifts it ~28%. Vendors with 5+ reviews convert ~23% better on average than those with fewer, get 2.2x the storefront views, and 1.9x the leads of vendors with no reviews at all.

We designed a new page with quick, clear shortcuts to the main review-collection actions, prioritizing the action vendors preferred most — Copy link — through a stronger CTA and a more prominent position. With many actions competing for space, we kept the design visually simple and easy to scan to reduce cognitive load.

Why it mattered

Reviews directly move the metrics that matter most: conversion, storefront views and leads. Making them harder to collect than necessary was leaving that performance upside on the table.

Key decision

Prioritize the single action vendors reach for most — Copy link — with a stronger CTA and top placement, and keep the rest of the page visually simple so it stays easy to scan despite the number of available actions.

Outcome

Time to request a review dropped by 50%, the volume of reviews collected rose by 35%, and conversion and engagement improved by 24%.

Dashboard update

This project used the Jobs-to-be-Done framework to enhance the vendor experience by guiding them through their planning journey. The goal was to drive higher conversion rates to ULS.

Client The Knot Worldwide · 2024
Responsibility Research · UX Design · Data Driven · Visual Design · Design Thinking
Area Growth & Conversion
Partners Add partners here

Increase ULS conversion by focusing the vendor dashboard on what each user actually needs next, instead of showing the same generic modules to everyone.

+42% Increased ULS conversion rate
+38% Improved dashboard engagement

The goal of this project was to increase ULS by focusing user attention and prioritizing the information shown above the default view on the vendor dashboard. We believed that applying the Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD) framework would increase conversion from dashboard users to ULS by offering them a path to follow through their planning journey.

The JTBD approach removes the focus from the product itself and places it on the customer and their needs. Applying it to the dashboard meant addressing three problems at once: personalization — the experience was nearly identical for every user regardless of which jobs they had completed; priorities & hierarchy — the dashboard offered every product with no clear path to follow; and positioning & interconnection — products were shown separately instead of connected to the user’s stage in their wedding planning journey.

The redesigned dashboard replaces the generic, one-size-fits-all modules with content ordered around the vendor’s actual progress — surfacing the next relevant action (guest list, budget, venue, vendor team) instead of a static list of every available product.

Why it mattered

Every user saw the same dashboard no matter what they had already completed, with no clear hierarchy or path guiding them toward the next step in their wedding planning.

Key decision

Apply the JTBD framework to personalize, prioritize and interconnect dashboard modules around each vendor’s planning journey, instead of listing every product with equal weight.

Outcome

ULS conversion rate increased by 42% and dashboard engagement improved by 38%.
