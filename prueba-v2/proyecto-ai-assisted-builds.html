Abora Tattoo

Abora Tattoo is an independent tattoo studio in Costa Teguise, Lanzarote, run by a team of five artists.

How it was built

Figma to Production, Vibe Coded Solo

A one-person workflow that turns a Figma identity into a bilingual, booking-ready site — validated against real client questions, tested before every change, and shipped in small steps.

1

Design in Figma

Build a dark, bold identity around the studio's name: a glowing sun motif behind a sculptural hero.

2

Build with AI

Code the site in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, working with AI as a development partner.

3

Validate the FAQ

Research the questions clients ask before booking and confirm every answer with the studio.

4

Build for Search & AI

Generate real Spanish and English pages, plus structured data, a sitemap and AI-crawler-friendly files.

5

Test Before Shipping

Run visual regression and functional tests before every change goes out.

6

Ship with Confidence

Ship in small, versioned steps on GitHub, ready to go live on the studio's own domain.

Abora Tattoo is an independent tattoo studio in Costa Teguise, Lanzarote, run by a team of five artists. Its online presence was an Instagram account and a Google Maps profile with 119 five-star reviews. That was enough for word of mouth, but not for the tourists searching "tattoo studio Lanzarote", many of them in English, on Google or, more and more, through AI assistants. The goal was a website with the studio's own identity that turns that search traffic into booking requests, works in two languages and gives search engines and AI models clear, trustworthy information to cite.

I started in Figma and built a dark, bold identity around the studio's name: Abora is the sun god of the ancient Canarians, which led to a glowing sun motif behind a sculptural hero figure. I then built the site myself in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, working with AI as a development partner. I researched the questions clients actually ask before booking (price, walk-ins, deposits, aftercare, minors, payment) and confirmed every answer with the studio, so the FAQ reflects real policy instead of filler.

Key decisions along the way:

  • The hero is a scroll-driven video.
  • The artist cards flip to show each artist's style and bio.
  • The booking form asks for exactly what the artists need to quote: style, size, body area and colour.
  • The Google Maps profile became the single source of truth for phone, hours and reviews, so the site never contradicts it.

The result is a fast, accessible, bilingual site:

  • Spanish by default, with real English pages at /en/.
  • Interactive team section, filterable gallery, real Google reviews, a 12-question FAQ and a validated booking form that sends straight to the studio's inbox.
  • Floating WhatsApp shortcut.
  • Structured data, a sitemap, AI-crawler-friendly robots.txt and llms.txt files, and GDPR-compliant legal pages with explicit consent on the form.

It was shipped in small, versioned steps on GitHub, with visual regression and functional tests before each change, and is ready to go live on the studio's own domain.

Why it mattered

For a small studio in a tourist destination, being found at the right moment matters. If a visitor can't quickly see the work, the prices and how to book, they book with someone else. Without a site, Abora depended entirely on platforms it didn't control.

Key decision

I chose real, separate pages for each language, generated by a small build script from one Spanish source, over a JavaScript language toggle. A toggle looks the same to a visitor, but search engines and AI models only read one language. Generated pages let both Spanish and English rank and be cited, while the studio maintains its content in a single place.

Outcome

The studio now has a site it owns, with a clear path from discovery to booking request in two languages. Everything is in place for Google and AI assistants to describe Abora correctly. The codebase is documented so future updates, such as new artists, photos or prices, take minutes.

The "Meet our team" section, introducing each artist's style and specialty

The booking form, asking for exactly what the artists need to quote

The FAQ, answering the real questions clients ask before booking

Real Google reviews, pulled in as the site's social proof

What scaled

The build script that generates full Spanish and English pages from a single source is reusable for any future bilingual or multi-language site. So is the content process behind it: researching the questions clients actually ask and confirming every answer with the business before it goes live, rather than writing FAQ copy from assumptions.
