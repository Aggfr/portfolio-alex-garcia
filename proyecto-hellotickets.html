Until recently, Hellotickets' business was built entirely on ticket sales for a large number of events, mainly across Europe and the US. To evolve the product and bring trip experiences into the catalogue for the first time, the company licensed Viator's API, gaining access to its entire product offering and the ability to start expanding the business beyond tickets. Making that integration real meant more than plugging in a feed: it required a major redesign of the website's information architecture to make room for a product type it had never carried before.

The hypothesis was that integrating Viator's API and redesigning the information architecture would significantly increase revenue and user engagement by expanding the catalogue to include trip experiences alongside event ticket sales. Since this was the first time the team was working in the travel experience market, we spent two months on research before touching the design, running 15 interviews with users from around the world to better understand why they chose a particular product or service, build real empathy for their experience, surface their pain points and information gaps, and understand their purchase behavior.

That research surfaced a consistent pattern: the market was crowded and standing out mattered, users carried real doubts about the activities they were considering, and they compared several options before buying, weighing price, free cancellation and language above everything else. They didn't want to register just to make a purchase, they ran the full funnel through to checkout just to see the final price and commissions, and they were willing to pay more on a site that gave them confidence. Those insights shaped a comprehensive redesign of the information architecture built around Viator's catalogue of trip experiences, aimed at a seamless experience that drives both engagement and conversion.

Why it mattered

Trip experiences were a product type Hellotickets had never sold before, and the existing information architecture had nowhere to put them. Without that redesign, the investment in Viator's API would have stayed a backend connection vendors couldn't discover or buy through, never turning into either revenue or engagement.

Key decision

Rather than bolting trip experiences onto the existing, ticket-focused structure, the information architecture was rebuilt around both product types from research, not assumption: two months and 15 interviews across markets shaped which paths and comparison points, price, free cancellation and language, the new structure had to support.
